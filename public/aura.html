"You exist in the field the moment you drop a folder in. No registration. No URL. Just presence."

AURA is a new protocol that runs beneath the browser, beneath HTTP, beneath everything you've been told the internet must be. Every participant is a folder. Every folder has an aura — a living semantic fingerprint built automatically from its contents.

When you seek something, the field resonates. No index server to query. No address to remember. Whoever matches, responds. The AI layer is not an add-on — it is the routing table.

Your entry point can be aura.py or aura.sh or aura.rs or anything. The folder runs itself when someone arrives. Think of index.html — except it's code, not markup, and it lives on Linux, not on a server.

in/
Files arrive here when a peer routes to you — by resonance, by name, by intent. You didn't give your address. The field found you. This is what "receiving a message" means in AURA.
out/
Drop a file here. The field watcher reads it, scores every peer, and copies it to the in/ of whoever resonates. No send button. No address. Files move by what they are.
shared/
Always visible to the field. Anyone can aura ls your shared/ or pull files from it. This is publishing without a server. Permanent presence in the field.
private/
Never scanned. Never indexed. Never announced. The field does not know it exists. Your keys, your journal, your work in progress — stay exactly where they are.
