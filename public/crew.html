HOW THE HARVEST WORKS

Every phase of a Marvenik mission — from approach to re-entry — is designed to operate without a single human aboard. Crew deployment is the last resort, not the plan.

01
APPROACH PHASE — SPECTRAL SURFACE SCAN

NEWTONIAN PRISM SCANNING

As the craft approaches 16 Psyche, it deploys a forward-facing spectral array — the same principle Isaac Newton applied when splitting starlight through a prism to reveal its elemental composition. Each surface patch reflects a unique spectral fingerprint: platinum-group metals, iron-nickel alloys, gold concentrations, and rare earths all emit distinct wavelength signatures. The onboard AI processes the full spectral return in real time, generating a high-resolution chemical density map of the entire visible surface before the craft has even entered orbit.

This is fast, passive, and requires zero physical contact. The craft scans thousands of square kilometres per hour. The output is a ranked list of extraction sites, sorted by yield-per-square-metre — the AI selects the top targets and plots the hop sequence autonomously.

EXTRACTION PHASE — THE HOE-RAKE ARM SYSTEM

CIRCULAR ARM ARRAY • AGGRESSIVE MATTER COLLECTION

The craft extends a radial array of mechanical arms outward from the hull — positioned in a full circle around the body. Each arm is fitted with dual-action end-effectors: a forward-raking blade that aggressively breaks and loosens surface regolith, and a hoeing scoop that drives matter inward toward the centre of the circle. The arms sweep rapidly, coordinating in sequence to avoid interference — continuously churning and driving material toward the craft's underbelly.

Central Vacuum Extraction

From the geometric centre beneath the craft, a high-flow vacuum tube extends into the collected matter. All loosened regolith, dust, and broken rock — already concentrated by the arm sweep — is drawn up this tube and into pressurised internal cargo containers. The system is self-clearing: the arms continuously replenish the intake zone as the vacuum empties it. No material is wasted and no human judgement is required mid-cycle.

HOP PHASE — MULTI-SITE SURFACE TRAVERSAL

JUMP — EXTRACT — REPEAT

Once a site is exhausted, the craft does not return to orbit. In Psyche's micro-gravity, a short thruster burn is enough to hop the vehicle several kilometres to the next pre-selected site — landing, deploying the arm array, and resuming extraction within minutes. The hop sequence was defined before touchdown by the spectral scan's ranked site list. The craft works through each site autonomously, maximising cargo density before committing propellant to the return journey.

RETURN PHASE — AUTONOMOUS EARTH DELIVERY

FULL CARGO — DIRECT RETURN

When cargo containers are at capacity, the craft executes a departure burn and initiates autonomous return trajectory. No crew. No rendezvous handoff needed. The sealed cargo pod re-enters on a pre-calculated recovery orbit, deploying deceleration systems for ocean or land recovery. Ground teams handle offloading. The craft is refuelled, inspected autonomously, and cleared for the next mission cycle without requiring human sign-off on standard telemetry.

A second Marvenik craft is routed to the stranded harvester's location, equipped with retrieval arms to physically capture and secure the disabled craft, then tow or transport it — and its cargo — back to Earth recovery orbit.
