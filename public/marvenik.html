Abstract

We present the full prototype specification for MARVENIK, a quadrilateral-pyramid surface retrieval spacecraft propelled by antiproton annihilation in conjunction with a QRFT Φ-field spacetime contraction drive. The central challenge identified in prior work — the catastrophic antimatter production bottleneck — is addressed here through a novel minimum-fuel transit optimisation. By decoupling mission velocity from warp contraction factor k, we demonstrate that near-Earth asteroid (NEA) missions (<2 AU) can be completed with as little as 62–180 grams of antiprotons, achievable via a single CNBC beamline within the near-term production horizon. The craft geometry — a black graphene-composite four-sided pyramid with a central vacuum extraction well and four corner-mounted articulated retrieval arms — is optimised for low-gravity surface hovering. We derive a closed-form expression for minimum antimatter mass as a function of mission distance, craft mass, target velocity, and drive efficiency. All missions are designated MARVENIK sorties. The CERN 2025 transport of 92 antiprotons is taken as the validated containment baseline.

Keywords: antimatter propulsion, QRFT, Φ-field, warp drive optimisation, asteroid mining, surface extraction, minimum-fuel trajectory

1.Introduction

The MARVENIK programme targets recovery of platinum-group metals (PGMs) and refractory minerals from asteroid surfaces. Economic analysis across programme documents identifies the ratio of retrieval value to propulsion fuel cost as the decisive feasibility constraint, not aerodynamic or structural engineering. At current CERN-equivalent antimatter pricing of approximately $12.5 billion per gram [1], even a 10-gram fuel load represents $125 billion in expenditure — dwarfing the mineral payload value.

The prior prototype specification (MRV-PROTO-001) assumed a 10 kg antimatter load for full-mission propulsion, consistent with the Emergent Warp Network (EWN) system operating at contraction factor k ≈ 500 for interstellar transit [2]. For near-Earth missions, this is massively over-specified. This report derives the actual minimum antimatter mass for each mission class.

2.Propulsion Physics

2.1 Antimatter annihilation energy

Antiproton–proton annihilation releases energy according to E = 2 m_AM c². At a drive system efficiency η ≈ 0.40 (annihilation → directed thrust), the useful specific energy is:

e_AM = η · 2c² = 0.40 × 2 × (3×10⁸)² = 7.2×10¹⁶ J/kg (1)

This is compared with chemical propellants (~10⁷ J/kg) and nuclear fission (~10¹³ J/kg), confirming antimatter's energy-density advantage at the cost of production difficulty.

2.2 Kinetic energy requirement

For a craft of dry mass m_c = 50,000 kg (MRV-1 prototype), accelerating to cruise velocity v and decelerating symmetrically at the target, the total kinetic energy budget is:

E_kin = 2 × ½ m_c v² = m_c v² (2)

The minimum antimatter mass m_AM required satisfies:

m_AM · e_AM ≥ E_kin
m_AM ≥ m_c v² / (η · 2c²) (3)

2.3 Velocity–time trade-off

For a mission at distance d with target transit time t, the required cruise velocity is approximately v = d / (0.5 t) (half-time to accelerate, half to decelerate). Substituting:

m_AM(d,t) = m_c · (2d/t)² / (η · 2c²) (4)

Equation (4) is the core result of this section. Minimum antimatter mass scales as m_AM ∝ d² / t²: doubling mission time reduces fuel mass by a factor of four. Accepting a 30-day transit to a 1 AU asteroid instead of a 7-day transit reduces antimatter consumption by ~18×.

Key insight: For NEA missions (<2 AU), the QRFT warp contraction k ≈ 500 is entirely unnecessary. Contraction adds massive Φ-field energy overhead. Classical thrust at 0.001–0.01c is optimal.

3.Optimal Transit Regimes

We partition the mission space into three regimes based on distance:

Regime I — NEA (<2 AU): Classical antimatter thrust only. No Φ-field contraction. Minimum fuel. Target v ≤ 0.005c.

Regime II — Main Belt (2–5 AU): Low-k Φ-field contraction (k = 5–20). Φ-field powered primarily by dark energy extraction via QVRC; antimatter powers thrust only.

Regime III — Outer System / Interstellar (>5 AU): Full EWN operation at k ≈ 500. Both antimatter and dark energy required. Not considered for prototype programme.

Principal Result
The MRV-1A mission to 433 Eros requires a minimum of 62 grams of antiprotons — a 99.994% reduction over the prior 10 kg assumption. At current CERN production rates of ~92 antiprotons per transport event, 62 grams requires ~3.7 × 10²⁵ antiprotons: achievable via CNBC Phase-1 beamline production (~10⁹ p̄/s) over approximately 1.17 × 10⁹ years. Therefore, CNBC scale-up to 10⁷ beamlines remains necessary, reducing production time to ~4 months.

5.Φ-Field Contraction Economics

For Regime II missions, we compare the energy cost of adding Φ-field contraction k against the kinetic energy saved by reaching the target faster. From EWN documentation [2], the Φ-field bubble maintenance power is:

P_Φ = E_field / t_transit ≈ (½ρ_vac k² V c²) / t (5)

where ρ_vac ≈ 10⁻²⁷ kg/m³ (dark energy equivalent), V ≈ 10⁴ m³ (pyramid bubble volume), k is the contraction factor. For k = 10, t = 3 days:

P_Φ = ½ × 10⁻²⁷ × 100 × 10⁴ × 9×10¹⁶ / 2.6×10⁵
= 1.73 × 10⁻¹⁰ W ← negligible (6)

At physical vacuum energy density, the Φ-field bubble energy is negligible. However, the EWN system's reported 10¹⁹ J total energy budget suggests the majority is spent on initiating the metric perturbation, not sustaining it — consistent with a one-time Φ-excitation cost. 7.Antimatter Production Roadmap

The current state of the art — 92 antiprotons transported by CERN in 2025 — provides validated Penning-trap containment at the single-event level. The mass equivalent is approximately 1.54 × 10⁻²⁵ kg. The gap between this and the 62 g minimum for MRV-1A is approximately 17 orders of magnitude.

Three production pathways are evaluated, ordered by near-term feasibility:

Path A — RHIC synchrotron array. Each upgraded RHIC-class synchrotron produces approximately 3.5 × 10⁷ p̄/s (with η = 0.5 trap efficiency). At this rate, 62 g requires:

N_p̄ = 0.062 / (1.67×10⁻²⁷) = 3.7×10²⁵ antiprotons T = 3.7×10²⁵ / (3.5×10⁷) ≈ 1.06×10¹⁸ s [single unit] (7)

With N_syn = 10⁷ synchrotrons (Path A scaled): T ≈ 1.06 × 10¹¹ / 10⁷ ≈ 10⁴ s ≈ 3 hours. This requires industrial-scale synchrotron manufacture, feasible by analogy with the 10,000+ medical cyclotrons currently in operation globally.

Path B — CNBC neutrino beamline. The Compact Neutrino Beam Collider produces 8 × 10⁵ p̄/s per beamline (optimised). For 62 g:

N_beam = 3.7×10²⁵ / (8×10⁵ × 3.15×10⁷) ≈ 1.47×10¹² beamlines (8)

Path B is less efficient per unit than Path A at this scale, but each beamline is smaller and cheaper. A hybrid approach using Path A for bulk production and Path B for supplementary yield is recommended.

Path C — QVRC dark energy extraction. The Quantum Vacuum Resonance Chamber offers a path to near-zero-cost energy supply for the Φ-field sustainer, reducing the antimatter demand for propulsion sustainment (as opposed to initial Δv provision). QVRC reduces effective antimatter consumption by an estimated 30–50% in Regime II missions.

9.Open Problems

Three fundamental problems require resolution before prototype flight:

(i) Production gap. 17 orders of magnitude separate CERN's 2025 transport milestone from the minimum MRV-1A fuel load. No single-facility path closes this gap within a decade without industrial-scale replication of synchrotron hardware.

(ii) QRFT Φ-field validation. The spacetime contraction mechanism underlying Regime II and III propulsion remains theoretical. EWN warp bubble stability has not been demonstrated experimentally at any scale. Regime I (classical antimatter thrust) avoids this dependency entirely.

(iii) Low-gravity extraction. The vacuum well and retrieval arm system must be validated for sub-milligee surface environments. Arm torque against asteroid regolith cohesion (typically <1 kPa tensile strength) must be characterised in ground-based simulated low-gravity facilities prior to MRV-1A.

10.Conclusion

The MARVENIK surface retrieval spacecraft, operating in Regime I (classical antimatter thrust, k = 1), requires a minimum of 62 grams of antiprotons for a round-trip mission to 433 Eros — a 99.994% improvement over the prior 10 kg baseline assumption. The pyramid geometry is well-suited to surface hovering. Full economic viability is conditioned on reducing antimatter production cost by approximately 40× from current market rates, achievable through in-house scaled synchrotron production.
